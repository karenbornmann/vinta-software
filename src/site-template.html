The care model behind the software

Quilted Health runs a network of clinics that partner with local hospitals to provide pregnancy care led by midwives. To support that work, it owns Maternity Neighborhood, an electronic health record (EHR): the system clinicians use to document and manage each patient’s care.

For the clinics
  • ♥Midwife-led pregnancy care
  • ⌂Partnership with local hospitals
  • ✚Preventive care
  • ◷Annual wellness visits
  • ✓Contraception counseling
In the software
  • ▤Maternity Neighborhood, Quilted’s own EHR
  • ♀Built for pregnancy and gynecological care
  • ✎Documenting each patient’s care
  • ◎Managing care across the clinic network

Why the old system had to change

63%
Industry context

Healthcare providers increasingly struggle with EHRs: 63% report information-overload stress.

Source: athenahealth survey. An industry figure, not a Quilted Health or Vinta result.

Maternity Neighborhood had more than 14 years of continuous development. It still delivered its core features, but its foundations reflected an earlier era of healthcare software.

⛓

Small changes took big effort

Nearly every part of the system depended on another.

⟲

Outdated libraries slowed it down

Old dependencies made it harder to evolve safely.

⇄

Data could not move freely

Without native FHIR, interoperability was limited.

The legacy interface. The stack ran on Python 2 and Angular 1, with custom-built solutions across frontend, backend and database.

Patch or rebuild

After a careful evaluation, modernizing the existing codebase proved more complex and risky than building a new solution.

Keep patching

Keep fixing a tightly coupled Python 2 and Angular 1 codebase built on custom solutions.

Rebuild

Start on a FHIR-native foundation that supports SOC 2 and HIPAA requirements and offers ready components, at the cost of a significant upfront investment.

Why Medplum

Quilted Health chose Medplum, an open-source platform for building healthcare applications, as the foundation for the new system.

Starting over was a significant investment, and it needed stakeholder support.
